01

Why design
is the work

AI is loud. It is fast, it can do almost anything, and it says so. Kūhaku takes that power and sets it in calm, so that a person meets a clear surface, not a machine.

The whole difficulty is balance. Quiet that hides what you need is not calm, it is fog. Function that shouts over the surface is not help, it is noise. Kūhaku lives on the narrow line between them.

So the question asked of every line, word and colour is never is it simpler? nor is it more useful? It is: is this the most useful thing that is still quiet?

03

Ten principles

  1. 1

    One thing speaks

    In a mark, a row, a screen: at most one thing is red. The rest is ink and space. If two things want to speak, one waits.

  2. 2

    Colour has a reason

    Red means this is yours, this is on, this needs you. A colour that cannot finish the sentence "it is coloured because…" goes.

  3. 3

    State changes in place

    A thing shows its state by changing itself, never by adding something beside it. The footprint stays the same.

  4. 4

    Space is a material

    Empty space is designed. Remove before you add. A gap is not an invitation.

  5. 5

    Cut, not moulded

    Lines end square and corners are sharp, like a sheet of steel cut clean. Curves only where the thing is truly round.

  6. 6

    Type carries the order

    Size and weight (200, 300, 400) make the hierarchy. Never bold for emphasis, never boxes, borders or badges.

  7. 7

    Quiet until needed

    Labels and controls come when you point at them and leave when you don't. Nothing explains itself twice.

  8. 8

    Day and night are equals

    Every mark is drawn and checked in both. Password and lock screens are always night.

  9. 9

    Calm motion

    Things rise and settle, a quarter of a second. Nothing bounces or spins for show. A slow pulse means "working", and only that.

  10. 10

    The effort is invisible

    If it looks simple but feels arbitrary, it is not finished. Simple is the result of the most thinking, not the least.

04

Paper, ink, seal

Kūhaku has three colours. Not a palette to choose from: three materials, each with one job, used in a fixed proportion.

Paper#f7f4f1 · by night #121212
Ink#111111 · by night #ececec
Seal#e0301e

Paper紙

Warm, unbleached, never pure white. It is the space of 空白 itself, and it is most of every screen.

--bg

Ink墨

Sumi, a black that is not quite black. Full for what you read and what is on; at 60% (muted) for things at rest; at 8% (faint) for hairlines and edges.

--fg · --muted · --faint

Seal朱

Shu, the vermilion of the hanko: the stamp that says this is mine. Placed once, it means yours, on, or needs you. Never decoration. By night it lifts to #f0705a so it glows without burning.

--accent

About ninety parts paper, nine parts ink, one part seal. If the seal grows past one part, it stops meaning anything.

The question of greena study, not a decision made in passing

Charging once added a fourth colour. The battery is a good test of the whole system, so here it is worked through properly. The bar is 18 by 6: track at 18% ink, fill in full ink, red when low. What should change while it charges?

A · Rejected

A bolt beside it

Says what the bar could say itself, and adds a column: the whole top row shifts. Breaks principle 3.

B · Rejected

Green fill

Changes in place, so it is better. But green is a fourth material with no story, and charging does not need you. It is the loudest thing on screen while nothing is wrong. Breaks principles 1 and 2.

C · Rejected

Seal fill

Stays in the three colours, but red already means low. The same colour cannot mean "needs you" and "all is well".

D · Proposed

The fill breathes

Ink stays ink. While charging, the fill slowly breathes, the same pulse that already means "working" across Kūhaku. Same size, same colours, and visible only to someone who looks. Red keeps its one meaning: low.

10

Method

For an icon, a panel or a page. Slow at first; it becomes the fast way.

  1. Name the one idea

    What is it, and what does it change, in a few words. A sentence with "and" is two things.

  2. Find the gesture

    Draw the object, then reduce it (chapter 07). Stop when one more cut would lose it.

  3. Build it on the grid

    24 units, live area 4 to 20, rhythm of 6. Straight lines, square ends, sharp corners. Curves only for things truly round.

  4. Give one stroke the seal

    Which line is the idea, or the thing that changes? That one turns red when on. If none is, it is a glyph: one ink.

  5. Set it among its neighbours

    Run the inventory and look at it in its row, at real size, by day and night. Weight and rhythm must match.

  6. Subtract

    Hide each part in turn. If it still reads without one, that part goes.

  7. Show every state

    If any state grows wider, adds a symbol or adds a colour, find a way for it to change in place.

  8. Live with it for a day

    Calm is felt over time, not in a screenshot. Then write the decision here.

Before anything is kept

  • One thing speaks, and it is the right thing.
  • Every colour can say why it is there.
  • Every state changes in place.
  • Square ends, sharp corners, on the grid.
  • It reads at real size, by day and by night.
  • It sits in its row as if it had always been there.
  • Nothing is left to take away.
  • It is still useful. Quiet never costs function.
